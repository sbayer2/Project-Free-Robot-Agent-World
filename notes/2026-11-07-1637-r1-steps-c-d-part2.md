**Reading: no leak.**

- **Per body, F_track ≤ the T0 equivalent at matched precision:** ball 0.966 vs 0.964 (noiseless T0 0.975); box position 0.888 vs 0.918.
- What F_track adds beyond position comes from two legitimate camera channels:
  - **(a) box orientation (m4): +0.054 on the box alone.** T0 holds it in the up vector; the noisy up columns are what the floor's readout can't use.
  - **(b) the detection bit differs from the window bit:** 1432 frames in-window but undetected (the ball occluded by or inside the box during contact) and 2626 detected though the centroid is out of window (partial ball at the edge). Both are visible to a camera.
- My joint T0 twin (pos + bit both bodies + noisy in-plane angle) reads only 0.936 < F_track: the same readout limit again (adding box columns dilutes the ball's signal for ridge / kNN), not missing information.
- The ball appearance is fixed (`rgba 0.2 0.2 0.2`) and the box finish is fixed: **no appearance channel to material.** Frames contain only silhouettes on black.

**Worth stating in §10.2a:** for the strike, **visibility timing alone carries most of the density** (window bit 0.932, detection bit 0.948): *when* the ball leaves the window or disappears into the box encodes its rebound speed, i.e. the mass ratio. That's legitimate kinematics, but it makes W directly part of the signal, not just resolution: a different W changes the exit times. One more reason W must be fixed before training and never tuned on model results.
